# Final synthesis

The most interesting aspect of map corrections upon revisits occurred in mission 2, where there were gaps along the curved path the robot took to head into the leftmost room. Upon revisiting these areas, it captured more, but it still had gaps in its path, especially at higher angular velocities. Another major limitation of both maps is gaps in mapping underneath tables and around objects, despite circling them. This leads to a major limitation: the robot still cannot know that every mapped wall is geometrically correct. Its map combines LiDAR observations with motion estimates, so odometry error can shift or duplicate structures. The final screenshots do not provide enough before-and-after evidence to prove that SLAM corrected drift during a revisit. For example, we know that mission 1 produced the outside of the house with greater accuracy. 
The data also shows the overall difference between the two runs. Mission 1 took 5 minutes and mapped 10.88% of its grid, with about 7.99 square meters of known area. Mission 2 took 15 minutes but increased the known fraction to 50.11% and the known area to about 78.68 square meters. Its speckle fraction was also lower, dropping from 11.86% in Mission 1 to 2.28% in Mission 2. The quality score increased from 26.2 to 88.5. These measurements suggest that Mission 2 produced a more complete and less fragmented map overall, even though Mission 1 appeared to outline parts of the outside walls more accurately.
